Quick Start Guide

Everything you need to build sectors, inspect 3D airspace, and run quizzes

Want a hands-on walkthrough?

Launch the interactive Demo Session to explore a pre-built sample airspace and take a guided tour of the basic functionality.

1. Map Background & Saving (Settings Menu)

  • Upload / Delete Map: Load an image or PDF chart when starting a new blank session, or open the Settings menu (gear icon) anytime to upload a new map image or delete the currently loaded map.
  • Save File / Load File: Located at the top of the Settings menu (gear icon), allowing you to export or import your entire setup (map background, sectors, and display settings) as a single JSON configuration file anytime.

2. Drawing, Snapping, Conflict Resolution & Airspace Priority

  • Draw: Left-click on the 2D map to start drawing a section of airspace. Click the red starting dot to close the shape and set its floor and ceiling altitudes. Press Delete to delete the last point, or right-click to cancel.
  • Snap Mode: Choose Corners (snaps to existing points and line intersections), Edges (snaps anywhere along existing borders), or None.
  • Insert & Adjust Points: While drawing a shape, hold Shift and click-and-drag anywhere along an already-drawn line segment to insert a new corner along that line, or Shift + Drag any existing corner to move it.
  • Interactive Conflict Resolution: If a new airspace volume is drawn in a way where it overlaps existing airspace (both laterally and vertically), the overlapping region is highlighted in red hazard hatching. Modify the shape of the newly drawn volume until the banner turns green, or click Edit Altitudes / Details to provide altitude separation between the conflicting volumes.
  • Priority Order: When volumes overlap, Military (when HOT or within an Always Active sub-stratum) overrides Approach, which overrides Center. Airspace volumes of differing types can overlap, and will use this priority order when determining which airspace a flight is within.

3. Sector List Organization & Mobile Panel

  • Inspect: Hover over any sector or sub-stratum in the sidebar to highlight it on the map.
  • Modify: Click a volume row to edit its altitudes or assigned sector.
  • Organize: Click the eye icon to temporarily hide the volume from the map (this only affects visuals while editing, not simulator airspace detection). Click the trash icon to delete the volume, or drag and drop the layers to reorder them in the sidebar.
  • Collapsible Panels: Collapse the sidebar on desktop (or use the bottom bar's expand/collapse chevron on mobile) whenever you want an unobstructed view of the radar scope.

1. Starting a Flight

Switch to Simulator mode to hide all of the sector boundaries and test your knowledge:

  • Random Flight: Automatically generates a flight path and altitude profile inside your active airspace.
  • Draw Route: Click (or tap on mobile) a start point and end point on the 2D map, then enter or randomize the start and end altitudes.
  • Military Airspace Control: Toggle military areas between HOT and COLD. HOT airspace will be displayed on the map. COLD airspace will be hidden on the map, unless the airspace is designated as Always Active and the Display Always Active on Map option is selected. The program can randomly assign the status of Military Airspace by clicking Randomize Hot/Cold.

2. Assessing the Flight Path & Flight Replay

  • Select each sector the aircraft enters in chronological order from start to finish, click Add to place the selected sector into the sequence, and click Submit Assessment to submit your guess.
  • After submitting, click Explanation & Flight Replay (2D / 3D) to reveal the airspace and scrub through the flight timeline in 2D or 3D. Use the Visible Airspace toggle (Sectors on Route or All Sectors) in the replay overlay to switch between viewing only the volumes your flight penetrated or the entire airspace map.
  • Collapsible Floating Overlays: On mobile screens, the Simulation Paused quiz card and Explanation & Flight Replay panel (which opens in a compact collapsed state by default on mobile) can be expanded or collapsed by tapping their headers to maximize radar visibility.

Cross Section View & Floating Cards

  • Cross Section View (Top-Right): Drag the altitude slider or use the -1k / +1k buttons (1,000-ft steps) to inspect a single altitude level. In 2D, it filters the map to sectors active at that altitude. In 3D, it slices off volumes above that level and outlines intersected footprints on a horizontal cutting plane.
  • Collapsible Control Cards: Click or tap the header of the Cross Section View or 3D Airspace Inspector panel to collapse or expand its sliders at any time.

About the Demo Airspace

How this tutorial differs from regular radar training

This demo session is designed to help you get familiar with the application's tools, 2D and 3D airspace views, and flight simulator quizzes.

Why Sector Boundaries Are Shown on the Demo Map

Because this demo uses a fictional airspace that you aren't already familiar with, it includes an annotated background image that outlines where the sectors, shelves, and military zones are located so you can immediately test the simulator without memorizing anything first.

How Regular Training Works

During regular use, this program is designed to be used with a blank map image—just the way it would be seen on an actual radar scope—and it will be up to you to determine the airspace boundaries and altitude strata on your own during simulator quizzes.
